A Kinect alternative for PC: motion games with the webcam you already own

October 2026 · 7 min read

If you are looking for a Kinect alternative for PC, the good news is that you may not need new hardware. Kinect read your body with a special camera. Today a normal webcam and a small model in the browser can do a large part of that job for games. This page explains what Kinect did, what is left, and where a webcam falls short.

We make BodyArcade, a free running game you control with your body in front of a webcam. We use it as the example, and we say plainly where a real depth camera still wins.

What Kinect did

Kinect was a motion sensor for Xbox that let you play with your body instead of a controller. The first model shone a near-infrared pattern into the room. The Xbox One model used time of flight, timing infrared light as it bounced back. Both gave the console a depth map: how far each part of the room was from the sensor.

From that depth map, the software picked out people and built a simple skeleton of joints for each player. Games read the skeleton. A raised arm, a jump or a lean became an input.

Sources, checked 22 October 2026: Wikipedia, Kinect.

What happened to Kinect

Microsoft stopped making Kinect for Xbox One on October 25, 2017. The adapter that connected it to the Xbox One S, Xbox One X and Windows PCs was dropped in January 2018. A developer version, Azure Kinect, came out in June 2019 at US$400. Microsoft discontinued it in October 2023.

So there is no Kinect you can buy new for gaming today. Used units and adapters still turn up, but there is no new hardware and very few new games for it.

How a webcam reads your body

A webcam only sees a flat picture. A pose model fills in the rest. Google's MediaPipe Pose Landmarker tracks 33 body landmarks, such as the nose, shoulders, hips, knees and ankles, in each frame. It is built to run on the device in real time, and it works inside a web page.

BodyArcade uses this model in your browser. A game does not need all the points. It watches a few stable ones, like your hips and shoulders, and measures how they move compared with your own standing height. Our guide to how webcam fitness games work goes into the details.

Sources, checked 22 October 2026: Google AI Edge, MediaPipe Pose Landmarker guide.

What a webcam can't do that Kinect could

  • Depth. One webcam cannot measure how far away you are. Steps toward the screen are a poor control.
  • Low light. Kinect brought its own infrared light. A webcam needs a lit room, with the light in front of you.
  • Groups. Kinect games could follow a few players at once. BodyArcade follows one person at a time, so friends take turns.
  • Small hand moves. Fast jabs and finger poses are hard to read from a normal video frame.

What a webcam does well is the large stuff: jumping, squatting, ducking and leaning. Those are the moves most Kinect sports and party games were built on anyway.

Playing Kinect-style games on a PC with a webcam

BodyArcade is a running game built around those big moves. You jump, duck, squat, and lean left or right to clear walls and bars on a road. A run lasts 90 seconds, and three misses end it early.

  1. Place the camera. Put the laptop or webcam at about hip height on a steady table.
  2. Step back. Stand about two metres away, so the camera sees you from head to hips with room above your head.
  3. Light the room. Face a window or a lamp. Do not stand with a bright window behind you.
  4. Open the game. Press Play with camera and allow the camera when the browser asks.
  5. Stand still for a second. The game learns your standing height, so a jump means the same for a child and a tall adult.
  6. Run. Move when the ring around each prompt turns yellow.

Questions people ask

Do I need a Kinect to play BodyArcade?

No. BodyArcade uses the webcam in your laptop or on your monitor, or the front camera of a phone. It runs in Chrome, Edge or Safari with nothing to install.

Can a webcam track two players like Kinect did?

Some webcam games can, but BodyArcade follows one person at a time. Players take turns and compare scores with a challenge link.

Will an old Kinect work as the camera?

BodyArcade only needs a normal camera feed, so any camera your browser can see will do. In practice a cheap USB webcam is the easier choice.
