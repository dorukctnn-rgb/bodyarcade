Indoor motion games for kids, played with a webcam

Updated 29 October 2026 · 5 min read

On a rainy day, a camera can turn a living room into a play space. BodyArcade is a set of 11 short games that a child plays by moving in front of a laptop, tablet or phone camera: squatting, reaching, leaning, punching. This page is for the adult who sets it up. It says which games have no jumping, what the room needs and what the app keeps about a child.

Which games to start with

The games are not graded by age, so you choose. 10 of the 11 games have a quiet mode with no jumping and no stamping, and every game has a seated version. These are the 8 free games, with the way to play each one without jumping:

Pad Work, Reaction Tag and Limbo Bend are Pro games. Kids active break, one of the Pro plans, puts 3 of them together for a break between lessons: Star Catch, Reaction Tag and Limbo Bend, about 9 minutes, with no jumping.

What the room needs

  • Space. About 2 metres clear around the player, on a floor that is not slippery. Move the coffee table.
  • Distance. For the standing games the camera must see your child from head to ankles: 2 to 3 metres from a laptop, or 1.5 to 2 metres from an upright phone. For 4 of the games, head to hips is enough, about 1 metre away.
  • Light. In front of the player or beside, not behind.
  • An adult nearby. Our terms ask children to play with an adult nearby.
  • The neighbours. Quiet mode is in Settings, and it is kind to the people downstairs.

Two children on one camera

On a big screen such as a laptop, a desktop or a TV, two players can share one camera in 8 of the 11 games. Player 1 stands on the left and Player 2 on the right, about 1 metre apart and about 3 metres from the screen, and each gets a score of their own. It is not offered on phone-sized screens. What the app keeps about a child

  • The video. The camera picture is turned into 33 body points on the device. It is never uploaded, recorded or saved.
  • No online profile under 16. The games work on the device, and the app sends nothing about the child to the friends service. Friends, rooms, public lobbies and weekly boards are not available.
  • No ads. There are no ads and no advertising trackers.
  • Results stay on the device. With the Family plan, up to 6 people on one device keep their own results, stored on that device only. A child’s profile never gets an online profile of its own.
  • Buying is for adults. Gumroad and Google Play have their own age rules for buying.

The privacy policy and the terms have everything in full.

What to know before you start

  • It can miss a move. One camera reads big moves of big body parts well, and a small child close to the camera or in poor light can be harder to read. When a move does not count, the results screen says why.
  • It is not medical advice. It does not measure heart rate and makes no claim about a child’s health.
  • It adds no resistance. The games are bodyweight movement. They suit a rainy afternoon, not a training plan.

Questions parents ask

What age is BodyArcade for?

Anyone can play on their own device, and the games have no age rating. Our terms ask children to play with an adult nearby. Online features (friends, rooms, public lobbies and weekly boards) are for players 16 and older.

What does BodyArcade send about my child?

Under 16 there is no online profile, and the app sends nothing about the child to the friends service. The video is never uploaded, recorded or saved. Read the privacy policy for the details.

Does my child need a camera?

The games are played with a camera. Games that allow it can also be played with the keyboard or by touch, but those rounds are practice and are not kept in the records.

Is it free?

8 of the 11 games are free, with their seated and quiet versions and no ads. Pro adds the other 3 games, more plans and harder levels. The Pro page and the prices page have the plans.

Can two children play together?

Yes, on a big screen: 8 of the 11 games let two players share one camera. Online rooms are for players 16 and older.
