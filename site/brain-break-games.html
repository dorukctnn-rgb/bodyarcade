Brain break games and PE games for the classroom, played with a webcam

Updated 29 October 2026 · 6 min read

A brain break is a short pause in a lesson to move. BodyArcade has 11 short movement games that start in a browser on the classroom computer. One student plays in front of the webcam, or two side by side, while the class watches the screen. Every game has a seated version, and 10 have a quiet mode, so nobody has to jump.

How to run a break from one screen

A camera game follows one player, or two side by side. The rest of the class watches the screen, and can copy the move from their seats. One way to set it up:

  1. Put the computer with the camera where the class can see the screen: on a projector or a classroom display.
  2. Clear about 2 metres in front of the camera, so the player has room and nobody walks through the picture.
  3. Open the page, choose a game and allow the camera. The camera check measures the player before a game, so each new player stands at it first.
  4. Let one student play a round, or two students side by side in the 8 games that allow it. A round lasts from 90 seconds to 7 minutes.
  5. Swap players and pick the next game.

Quiet and seated: nobody has to jump

Every game has a seated version, and 10 of the 11 have a quiet mode with no jumping and no stamping, which keeps the noise down. Both are switches in Settings.

Ready-made breaks

A workout plan is a short run of games with a rest between them. Two of them are made for a break between lessons.

  • Desk break (free): 2 seated games, Twist Turbine and Star Catch, about 6 minutes, with 10 seconds of rest.
  • Kids active break (Pro): 3 short games with no jumping, Star Catch, Reaction Tag and Limbo Bend, about 9 minutes, with 10 seconds of rest between them. The app describes it as a break between lessons.

What stays on the computer

  • The video. The computer turns the camera picture into 33 body points on the spot. The video is never uploaded, recorded or saved.
  • No accounts. Nobody has to sign in or give an e-mail address to play the free games.
  • Nothing online for younger students. Under 16 there is no online profile at all. The games work on the device, and the app sends nothing about the player to the friends service.
  • No ads. There are no ads and no advertising trackers.

The privacy policy lists what BodyArcade keeps and for how long. We cannot tell you whether it fits your school’s rules for web tools, so check them first.

What the app does not do for a class

  • It has no class tools. There is no roster, teacher dashboard or school licence, and results stay on the device.
  • It is not a PE curriculum. It is a set of short movement games, useful as a break, a warm-up or a reward.
  • The camera reads one player. Two players can share a camera in 8 of the 11 games. A whole class cannot play at once.
  • It can miss a move. One camera reads big moves of big body parts well, and light and distance matter. The set-up guide has the details.
  • Children play with an adult nearby. That is what our terms ask, and it fits a lesson.

The terms give each person a personal right to use BodyArcade. If you want to use it with classes regularly, write to info@peakappsstudio.com and tell us what you need. How webcam fitness games work has the set-up for a room.

Questions teachers ask

Is BodyArcade free for teachers?

8 of the 11 games are free for everyone, with their seated and quiet versions, and the Desk break plan is free too. Pro adds the other 3 games and the other plans. There is no separate teacher plan. The prices page has the plans.

Do students need an account?

No. Nobody needs an account, a password or an e-mail address to play. An online profile exists only for friends and rooms, and nobody under 16 can make one.

Can the whole class play at once?

No. The camera reads one player, or two players side by side in 8 of the games. The rest of the class can watch the screen and copy the move at their seats.

Does it need special equipment?

A computer with a camera and a current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari), and a screen the class can see. Check with your school that the camera and the site are allowed on that computer.

Is the camera video recorded?

No. The video is read on the computer and is never uploaded, recorded or saved.
